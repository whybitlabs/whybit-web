T-01 the explorer

the first lun-e. off-the-shelf modules wired together inside a printed tank: a dev board, two continuous servos, a charger, a booster and a switch. it proved the idea — and showed what to fix: servos that creep at neutral, a booster that sags, and a lot of wires. the original build page is kept below, unchanged.

/ v1 · 03 — wiring

four signal wires and one power chain.

signal
oled · sda gpio 4
oled · scl gpio 3
left servo · signal gpio 5
right servo · signal gpio 6
power, in order
  1. 01 li-ion cell → tp4056 B+ / B−
  2. 02 tp4056 output → power switch
  3. 03 switch output → sx1308 input
  4. 04 sx1308 output → esp32-c3, oled, both servos
  5. 05 every module shares one common ground

set the sx1308 to the right output voltage before you connect anything to it. servos will happily brown out an under-fed rail.

/ v1 · 06 — the honest bit

what it does, and what it doesn't.

it can
  • +drive forward, back, arc and pivot in place on differential tracks
  • +take live throttle and turn commands over esp-now
  • +blink, glance around, and shift its expression as it moves
  • +run untethered on one rechargeable li-ion cell
  • +be printed, rebuilt and remixed from open files
it can't — yet
  • −drive itself — there are no distance or line sensors on board
  • −work out of the box without a transmitter: the controller firmware isn't published yet
  • −be driven from a phone app — esp-now talks to an esp32, not to bluetooth
  • −climb much: continuous-rotation servos are for desks and floors, not stairs
things that will bite you
servo neutral drift

continuous rotation servos rarely stop dead at nominal neutral. expect to tune it for your servos, or watch the robot creep when it should be still.

power stability

depends on the health of your cell and where the sx1308 is set. a sagging rail shows up as stuttering servos and a flickering screen.

v2
october 2026design study

the esp32-s3 camera board

the first try at putting all of lun-e on one board — and then some. a camera, a speaker and a microphone joined the motor driver and charger on a 40 × 25 mm, two-layer board with parts on both sides. it was laid out but never routed — seventy parts on two layers is dense — and the next version kept the single-board idea and dropped the extras.

brain
esp32-s3-wroom-1 n16r8 · psram for the camera
camera
ov2640 on a 24-pin fpc
audio
max98357a speaker amp + sph0645 i2s mic
motors
drv8833 → 2× small gear motors
power
tp4056 1 a charger, power path, tps63001 buck-boost
board
40 × 25 mm · 2 layers · 70 parts, both sides
what it taught
  • keptone board for everything, the drv8833 motor driver
  • droppedcamera, speaker and mic — for now
  • nextv3: the same one-board idea, with far fewer parts
v3
october 2026current

Lun-E RC

the version being built now. a custom 26 × 42 mm esp32-c3 board replaces every module from v1; n20 gear motors replace the servos; the tracks are 10 mm printed chain running on sprockets and bearing-mounted freewheels.

  • fixedservo creep at neutral → geared dc motors with a real driver
  • fixedbooster sag → cell feeds the driver directly, ldo for 3.3 v
  • fixedmodule wiring → one board, one usb-c port
  • newheadlights, wake button, deep sleep
