Lun-E

a palm-sized tracked rover on its own esp32-c3 board. two n20 gear motors, printed chain tracks, esp-now control — and it still looks back at you with animated oled eyes.

esp32-c3-mini-1 custom 26 × 42 mm pcb 2× n20 + drv8833 esp-now 0.96″ oled 1s lipo + usb-c 3d printed

/ 01 — how it drives

two tracks, four moves.

there is no steering mechanism. the difference in speed between the left and right track is the steering. each side has one n20 gear motor turning an 8-tooth sprocket; the far end of the track rides on a freewheel spinning on a 625-2z bearing. fifteen printed links of 10 mm chain wrap the two.

both forward
drives straight ahead
both reverse
backs up straight
one side slower
arcs toward the slow track
opposite directions
pivots in place, on the spot
drive
n20 → 8-tooth sprocket

one motor per side, on diagonally opposite corners. the sprocket presses straight onto the d-shaft.

idler
freewheel on a 625-2z

the bearing sits on a 5 mm stub moulded into the base; the freewheel's closed face hides it from outside.

track
10 mm printed chain

15 links per side. sprocket teeth drop into the window of each link; the links hinge on printed pins.

/ 02 — the board

one board. no more wiring.

the dev board, charger module, booster and switch from the first build are now one 26 × 42 mm, two-layer pcb. usb-c charges the cell and programs the chip; the cell feeds the motor driver and a 3.3 v ldo directly.

brain
esp32-c3-mini-1-h4 · risc-v, wi-fi + ble, 4 mb flash
motors
drv8833 dual h-bridge → 2× n20 gear motors
charging
tp4054 · 500 mA from usb-c · red led while charging
3.3 v rail
rt9080 ldo · 600 mA, 2 µA quiescent
cell
1s lipo, 300 mAh, protected · soldered to pads underneath
usb
vertical through-hole usb-c · charge + program
face
0.96″ ssd1306 oled on a 4-pin i2c header
lights
two white 0603 headlights
input
one button · wakes the board from deep sleep
board
26 × 42 mm · 2 layers · 1.6 mm
power, in order
  1. 01usb-c 5 v → tp4054 charger
  2. 02tp4054 → 1s lipo (protected cell)
  3. 03cell → drv8833 motor supply
  4. 04cell → rt9080 → 3.3 v
  5. 053.3 v → esp32-c3, oled, headlights

/ 04 — pinout

what every gpio does.

gpio 2 and 8 are strapping pins, so their 10k pull-ups double as the oled's i2c pull-ups. close jumper jp1 to hold gpio 9 low and boot into download mode.

gpiofunction
0motor a · ain2
1left headlight (low = on)
2oled i2c scl · strap pull-up
3free
4motor a · ain1
5button (active low, deep-sleep wake)
6 / 7motor b · bin1 / bin2
8oled i2c sda · strap pull-up
9strap · jp1 = download mode
10motor sleep (high = motors on)
18 / 19usb d− / d+
20right headlight (low = on)
21free (boot-log tx)

/ 06 — firmware

still esp-now. new sketch coming soon.

lun-e rc is driven the same way as the first build: an esp32 transmitter sends throttle and turn over esp-now. the firmware for this board — motor driver, headlights, button, deep sleep — is being written and will land here and on github.

/ 07 — the honest bit

what it does, and what it doesn't.

it can
  • + drive, arc and pivot on two geared tracks
  • + take throttle and turn over esp-now
  • + charge and reprogram over one usb-c port
  • + switch its headlights on and off
  • + sleep deep and wake on a button press
it can't — yet
  • − drive itself: there are no distance or line sensors
  • − run from a phone: esp-now talks to another esp32
  • − run the new firmware: it isn't published yet
things that will bite you
  • hand-soldered driver. the drv8833 is fitted by hand (tssop-16, 0.65 mm pitch).
  • cell first. the battery must be connected to charge or program the board.
  • a little slack. 15 links leave about 2 mm of slack in each chain.
  • upward light. 0603 leds shine up more than forward.
